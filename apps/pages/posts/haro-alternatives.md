HARO is active again, but it is no longer the only sensible place to find media requests. The best HARO alternative depends on the job: Source of Sources is closest to the old free email digest, Qwoted adds a vetted marketplace and tracking, MentionMatch narrows requests to B2B expertise, and ResponseSource is built around UK media enquiries.

There is also a second category worth separating from journalist-request platforms. If your goal is to make already-published articles available to AI writing agents whenever they need a source, a persistent source index such as CiteGuild fits better than another deadline-driven pitch inbox.

This guide compares seven current options using the same criteria: who sends the request, who responds, how opportunities arrive, public pricing, and the limitation that matters most.

## First, HARO is back—and Featured now owns it

The short timeline matters because many comparison pages still describe HARO as permanently closed.

Cision discontinued HARO and Connectively in December 2024. In April 2025, Cision [sold HARO to Featured](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cision-announces-sale-of-help-a-reporter-out-haro-to-featuredcom-302427990.html), and Featured [relaunched the free HARO email service](https://blog.helpareporter.com/category/news/). In May 2026, Featured [revived Connectively](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/26/3301366/0/en/Featured-com-Revives-Connectively-Launches-AI-Co-Pilot-for-PR.html) as the platform interface around those requests.

Today, the free HARO digest and the Connectively dashboard are two ways into the same broad request stream. Connectively says members receive HARO queries in real time, while email subscribers receive three daily digests. That makes Connectively a practical alternative to the old inbox workflow, even though it is operated by HARO's owner.

## How to choose: request timing and source consumer

Most HARO alternatives lists compare feature counts. A more useful decision starts with two questions.

1. **Is the opportunity reactive or persistent?** Journalist-request platforms are reactive: you wait for a relevant brief and answer before its deadline. A persistent source index makes existing work retrievable whenever a writer needs it.
2. **Who consumes the source?** HARO-style services connect human journalists or content writers with human experts. Agent-native retrieval lets a writing agent search published material during its drafting workflow, while retaining editorial control over whether to cite it.

That produces three distinct jobs:

- Choose a request service when you can respond quickly and want earned-media interviews, quotes, or mentions.
- Choose a niche request service when broad digests create too much irrelevant work.
- Choose a persistent index when you already publish source-worthy material and want it available beyond short request windows.

These jobs can complement each other. A founder might answer a Qwoted request personally while making the company's technical articles available through [sitemap-backed article indexing](https://citeguild.lvtd.dev/#article-indexing).

## 1. Source of Sources: best free replacement for classic HARO

[Source of Sources](https://www.sourceofsources.com/) is the closest match to the old HARO email experience. Peter Shankman, who founded HARO, runs the service and sends journalist queries by email up to three times a day. Sources reply directly when they have relevant expertise.

**Best for:** Experts who want a free, simple request digest without learning another dashboard.

**Pricing:** Free. The official site says neither sources nor journalists pay to participate.

**Why choose it:** The workflow is familiar, the rules strongly discourage off-topic pitching, and journalists using personal email addresses are verified before their first query is distributed.

**Tradeoffs:** There is no searchable opportunity dashboard, pitch tracker, or persistent source library. You still need to monitor email, judge fit quickly, and reply before the deadline. Source of Sources wins on simplicity; it is less suitable for teams that need reporting and workflow controls.

## 2. Qwoted: best for a vetted marketplace and pitch tracking

[Qwoted](https://www.qwoted.com/) connects media professionals with experts and PR representatives inside a structured platform. Accounts go through an individual vetting process, and paid plans remove the free plan's pitch delay.

**Best for:** Experts and PR teams that want alerts, profiles, media information, and tracked pitches in one place.

**Pricing:** Qwoted's [official pricing page](https://www.qwoted.com/pricing-toggle/) lists Basic as free with two pitches per month and a two-hour delay. Pro is $149 monthly or $99 per month when billed annually, with 35 monthly pitch credits and no delay. Team and managed plans use custom pricing.

**Why choose it:** Qwoted offers more operational structure than an email digest. The free plan is enough to inspect the workflow; Pro is designed for people who pitch regularly and care about speed.

**Tradeoffs:** The free allowance is restrictive, and the two-hour delay matters when journalists are working to short deadlines. Pro is also a meaningful expense for a solo expert who only sees a few relevant requests each month. Qwoted wins when its filtering and tracking save more time than the subscription costs.

## 3. Connectively: best for real-time HARO access

[Connectively](https://www.connectively.us/haro) is Featured's platform experience for HARO queries. Featured owns HARO and says Connectively members see queries when they go live, before the email digests are sent.

**Best for:** Former HARO users who still want HARO opportunities but prefer real-time alerts, profiles, and a dashboard.

**Pricing:** Free to join, with no credit card required, according to the current HARO-on-Connectively page.

**Why choose it:** It addresses the main inconvenience of the free digest: scanning batches of email after opportunities have already been open. The platform adds keyword alerts, pitch tracking, placement reports, and public expert profiles.

**Tradeoffs:** Connectively improves access and organization, but it does not change the reactive nature of the work. You need a relevant query, a credible response, and an editor who chooses to use it. It should be evaluated as a faster HARO interface, not as a source of guaranteed coverage or links.

## 4. MentionMatch: best for B2B and SaaS experts

[MentionMatch](https://mentionmatch.com/)—formerly Help a B2B Writer—matches requests from B2B writers to sources by expertise area. Its current categories include marketing, SaaS, sales, SEO, content marketing, analytics, AI and technology, ecommerce, and related fields.

**Best for:** SaaS founders, operators, and specialists who want fewer but more relevant B2B quote requests.

**Pricing:** Free for writers and sources. The previous Help a B2B Writer site and current MentionMatch registration flow both describe free participation.

**Why choose it:** Requests are routed only to matching expertise groups instead of being broadcast to a general list. Writers can ask for quotes, data, and specific examples; sources receive opportunities closer to their actual work.

**Tradeoffs:** Its focus is also its boundary. MentionMatch is a strong fit for B2B content, but it will not replace a broad media network for consumer, travel, health, or local-interest coverage. Sources still need to send specific, experience-backed answers rather than generic commentary.

## 5. SourceBottle: best for regional and consumer call-outs

[SourceBottle](https://www.sourcebottle.com/what-is-source.asp) connects journalists, writers, and bloggers with sources through public call-outs and targeted email alerts. The company was founded in Australia and [says its network covers](https://www.sourcebottle.com/about.asp) Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, the United Kingdom, Ireland, and North America.

**Best for:** Sources interested in regional, consumer, lifestyle, case-study, and product-related media opportunities.

**Pricing:** Posting journalistic call-outs and receiving general subscriber alerts are free. SourceBottle separately charges for certain requests involving samples, prizes, giveaways, or case studies for media pitches.

**Why choose it:** Sources can select topics, receive the Drink Up! alert, and browse call-outs publicly. Its geographic history makes it worth checking when a US-centered HARO list does not reflect your market.

**Tradeoffs:** Opportunity relevance depends heavily on your location and beat. The service mixes conventional expert requests with case-study and product-oriented opportunities, so subscribers need to filter carefully. SourceBottle wins for some regional and consumer categories; it is not automatically the best general-purpose option.

## 6. ResponseSource: best for UK media enquiries

[ResponseSource](https://www.responsesource.com/pr/journalistenquiry/) distributes verified enquiries from UK journalists and other media professionals to PR and communications subscribers. Its quality-control team reviews requests, and subscribers choose from 25 subject categories.

**Best for:** UK-focused companies and PR teams that need a managed feed of media enquiries in defined sectors.

**Pricing:** The [official pricing page](https://www.responsesource.com/pr/pricing/) lists annual Journalist Enquiry Service subscriptions from £625 per category, excluding 20% VAT. Journalists can submit enquiries for free.

**Why choose it:** The product is explicit about its UK focus, request verification, category filters, and office-wide licence. It is more targeted than a general free digest for teams with a repeatable UK media-relations program.

**Tradeoffs:** The annual category pricing is a poor fit for casual pitching or broad experimentation. It also serves PR and communications workflows rather than persistent discovery of published articles. ResponseSource wins when UK request quality and team access justify a paid subscription.

## 7. CiteGuild: best for persistent discovery by writing agents

[CiteGuild](https://citeguild.lvtd.dev/) is not a journalist-request marketplace. It is an AI-native editorial source network for publishers who want their existing articles available when writing agents need relevant evidence or context.

Members submit public sitemaps. CiteGuild indexes eligible articles and exposes the same relevance-ranked search contract through MCP, CLI, and API. A writing agent searches the corpus during drafting and decides whether a result helps the reader. CiteGuild later records member-to-member links it observes, without claiming it caused them.

**Best for:** SaaS companies, developer-tool publishers, independent blogs, and content teams that already publish useful articles and use writing agents.

**Pricing:** [CiteGuild membership is $10 per month](https://citeguild.lvtd.dev/pricing) for unlimited legitimate sitemap-backed sites. There is no free plan or trial.

**Why choose it:** One sitemap submission creates an always-on source surface. Articles can be found after the moment of publication, without waiting for a journalist to send a matching request. The [relevance-ranked agent retrieval](https://citeguild.lvtd.dev/#agent-retrieval) workflow fits directly into assisted writing.

**Tradeoffs:** CiteGuild does not arrange interviews, distribute journalist queries, run outreach, or guarantee citations. It requires source-worthy published content and an agent that chooses to search and evaluate results. If your goal is a personal quote in tomorrow's newspaper, use a request platform. If your goal is persistent discovery of your article library, CiteGuild addresses a different problem.

## Which HARO alternative should you choose?

Choose the service that matches your actual work, not the longest feature list.

- **Choose Source of Sources** if you want a free, familiar email digest and can respond quickly.
- **Choose Qwoted** if vetting, pitch intelligence, and workflow tracking justify a paid plan.
- **Choose Connectively** if you want HARO queries with real-time dashboard access.
- **Choose MentionMatch** if your expertise and target publications are firmly B2B.
- **Choose SourceBottle** if regional, consumer, case-study, or lifestyle call-outs fit your market.
- **Choose ResponseSource** if UK media enquiries are important enough for an annual category subscription.
- **Choose CiteGuild** if your durable asset is a library of published articles and your consumer is a writing agent.

A small combination is often more rational than a single winner. Use one broad or niche request platform for timely expert commentary, then use a persistent index for your evergreen content. Keep the measurement honest: track replies, accepted quotes, published mentions, referral traffic, and [detected citations](https://citeguild.lvtd.dev/#citation-observation) separately. A submitted pitch is not a backlink, and an indexed article is not a promised citation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is HARO still active in 2026?

Yes. Featured acquired HARO from Cision in April 2025 and revived the free email digest. Featured also operates Connectively, where members can access HARO queries through a dashboard.

### What is the best free HARO alternative?

Source of Sources is the closest free replacement for the classic email-digest workflow. MentionMatch is a stronger free fit when your expertise is specifically B2B, SaaS, marketing, sales, or technology.

### Which HARO alternative is best for UK media requests?

ResponseSource is the clearest UK-focused option in this list. It distributes verified journalist enquiries by category through an annual paid subscription for PR and communications teams.

### Does a HARO alternative guarantee backlinks?

No. A relevant, accurate pitch can improve your chance of being quoted, but the journalist or writer controls whether your contribution is used and whether the final article includes a link.

### How is CiteGuild different from HARO?

HARO and most alternatives distribute time-sensitive requests from journalists or writers to human experts. CiteGuild indexes opted-in articles so writing agents can retrieve relevant published sources through MCP, CLI, or API while drafting.
